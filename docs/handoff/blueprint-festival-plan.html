Blueprint Festival

Run a UK music festival across a three-day weekend. Lay out the site on a farm, book the bill, then keep sixty thousand people fed, dry, safe and happy when the rain comes in on Saturday night.

What the player does

You are the festival director. A year is one festival: months of planning squeezed into a few screens, a build week, then the three show days played out in real time like a hospital shift. Each year the licence, the site and the crowd get bigger.

Winter

Plan

Set the capacity you are applying for, ticket prices and tiers, and book acts against a budget.

Build week

Lay out

Drag out stages, camping fields, trader rows, toilets, trackway and fencing. Hire crews and stewards.

Fri to Sun

Run the show

Gates, queues, crowd flow, weather, medical calls, set changeovers and curfew, hour by hour.

Monday

Tear down

Litter pick, damage to the farmer's fields, the final accounts and the reviews.

Next year

Grow

A good year earns a bigger licence, better acts returning your calls and a second site option.

Festival colours, same house style

The layout stays the "Clinical light" one from the hospital: light panels, top bar, bottom build bar. Only the colours change, as School went green and gold and Supermarket teal and tangerine. Festival takes stage pink and sunflower, with red, amber and green kept strictly for safety status.

Laying out the site

Building works like the hospital: drag to size, minimum sizes per type, furniture placed for you. Rooms become zones and corridors become trackway. Nothing comes staffed.

Arena

  • Main stage, big top, small stages
  • Front barrier and pit, sound desk tower
  • Each stage has a safe capacity from its field size

Camping

  • General, family, quiet and glamping fields
  • Fire lanes, water points, showers
  • Pitches fill by ticket type from Thursday

Traders

  • Food trucks, bars, merch, market stalls
  • You rent pitches; busy spots earn more
  • Needs power, water and waste runs

Welfare

  • Toilets and handwash by headcount
  • Medical tent, welfare tent, lost children
  • Free water, shade and info points

Back of house

  • Artist area, production office, crew catering
  • Generators, cable runs, water tanks
  • Emergency vehicle routes kept clear

Edges

  • Perimeter fence, entrance lanes, box office
  • Car parks and shuttle bus stop
  • Flags, lights and signs for wayfinding

Booking the bill

Acts are fictional, generated with a genre, a fee, a draw (how many ticket-holders want to see them) and an energy level. The timetable is a grid of stages against hours, the festival's version of the hospital rota.

  • Draw against field size. A big name on a small stage overfills the field. That is the main cause of crush risk, and it is your own doing.
  • Clashes. Two popular acts at once splits the crowd, which is safer but annoys fans of both. One act alone moves everyone at once.
  • Changeovers and curfew. Stage crew numbers set changeover time. Overrun past the licence curfew and the council fines you.
  • Riders and no-shows. Late acts, storms and illness force a reshuffle on the day.

Crowd flow and safety

This is the heart of the game, the equivalent of triage. Every person walks the site using the hospital's pathfinding, and the game measures people per square metre everywhere, live.

StatusPeople per m²What happensWhat you can do
Comfortableunder 2People move freely and reach bars and toiletsNothing
Busy2 to 4Slow movement, more faints, tempers shortenSend stewards, open a relief exit, show a screen message
Crush riskover 4Injuries start; left alone it becomes a major incidentClose the field, pause the act, pull people over the barrier

Pinch points matter as much as stages: a narrow gate between two fields at changeover, the bridge to the campsite at 11pm, the entrance on Friday morning. Barrier layout, one-way routes and steward posts are the tools.

Weather: rain, mud and heat

A forecast arrives a week out and gets more accurate each day, so you choose how much trackway and straw to pay for before you know. Ground has a wetness value per tile, and footfall turns wet grass into mud.

What each kind of weather does

  • Rain. Mud on busy routes, stuck vehicles, tents flooding in low ground, more people sheltering in bars and big tops.
  • Heat. Dehydration and heat exhaustion, water points overwhelmed, bar income up.
  • Wind. Above a set speed, high stages and the wheel must close.
  • Lightning. Show stop and shelter. The hardest call in the game.

Counter-moves: trackway, straw, woodchip, pumps, free ponchos, shade sails, extra water.

The medical tent borrows from the hospital

The medical tent is a small A&E under canvas, running on Blueprint Hospital's existing rules: red, amber and green triage, live observations on monitors, staff walking to patients, and conditions kept in the same JSON format so the medical editor still works.

Festival conditions

Dehydration, heat exhaustion, sprains and fractures from mud, cuts, sunburn, bee stings, asthma, allergic reactions, too much alcohol, hypothermia on a wet night, crush injuries.

Who works there

First aiders handle green. Paramedics, nurses and a doctor take amber and red. Roving response teams bring casualties in by wheelchair or stretcher from the pit.

Transfer out

Anything the tent cannot treat goes by ambulance to the local hospital. Each transfer takes a vehicle off site for a while and counts in the council's debrief.

Welfare, not medical

Lost children, lost phones, people who are cold, upset or overwhelmed. A good welfare tent keeps them out of the medical queue.

Possible later link: load a saved Blueprint Hospital as "the local hospital" so festival transfers arrive at your own A&E. Nice to have, not part of the first version.

Money and reputation

Income

  • Tickets: early bird, general, day, family, glamping
  • Trader pitch fees and a share of bar sales
  • Parking, lockers, merch, sponsors

Costs

  • Act fees, usually the largest
  • Staging, power, toilets, fencing hire
  • Staff, security, medical cover, licence
  • Land rent and repair of the fields

Scores

  • Crowd happiness, hour by hour
  • Safety record and council rating
  • Neighbours: noise, traffic, litter
  • Press reviews and next year's sales

Most of the money is committed before the gates open, so a festival can be lost in winter and only found out in July. Weather insurance is an optional cost each year.

What carries over from Blueprint Hospital

  • The 3D world. Orthographic camera, four view angles, day and night lighting, cutaway.
  • People. Style A characters, the look generator, walking, sitting, carrying, wheelchairs and stretchers.
  • The simulation core. Plain C# tested outside Godot, the clock and speeds, tile pathfinding, save and load.
  • Systems with a new skin. Triage and observations, hiring panel and shifts, toilets, cafeteria and vending as food traders, cleaners as litter pickers, tannoy as stage and site announcements, career levels as festival years.
  • The UI kit. Top bar, bottom build bar, panels, notifications, finances.

New work: outdoor terrain with wetness and mud, crowd density, the act booking and timetable screens, weather, stages and sound, and large crowds drawn cheaply. The mock-ups here drew about 1,800 people without special tricks; real festival numbers will need crowd figures simplified at a distance.

Questions before anything is built

  1. Scale. Start as a 5,000 person boutique festival and grow, or go straight to a 60,000 person giant?
  2. How real should the bad days be? Crushes and serious injuries shown plainly as in the hospital, or kept lighter?
  3. Player role. Festival director doing everything, or hire a site manager who recommends, as the Headteacher does in School?
  4. Camping. In the first version, or a later stage?
  5. Colours and name. Happy with pink and sunflower, and "Larkmoor" as the default festival name?
  6. Order. Where does this sit against School and Supermarket?
